# Design system

`app/globals.css` owns all visual tokens. TemmsTech uses cool off-white content surfaces, deep navy feature sections, and a two-tone brand blue (bright cyan `--brand` + deeper blue `--visual-highlight`, both sampled from the logo) for emphasis and primary actions on dark surfaces. Use semantic utilities, not raw colors. `.dark-surface` scopes semantic tokens for dark sections; this is an editorial surface treatment, not a user-selectable dark mode. Preserve the logo mark and wordmark. Cyan is for emphasis on dark surfaces; use the darker card accent for small text on white. Neutral tones may evolve within this blue family; do not reintroduce the old lime/green palette.

## Visual language

- Use the system sans font for the site and the mono font for small section labels. `hero-title` and `section-title` use fluid theme typography. Body is `text-base leading-7`, supporting copy is `text-sm leading-7`. Keep one h1 and ordered headings. Serif typography appears only inside decorative sample-product previews.
- `page-container` owns shared width and responsive gutters; `section-space` owns section padding. Use Tailwind spacing 2/4 within groups, 6/8 between groups, and the section utility between major ideas. Keep text at readable line lengths.
- Give each main section one distinct job. The homepage flow is proposition → capabilities → previous work → process → contact. Sample concepts live on /work only. Fold related content into an existing section instead of repeating the company pitch.
- Use reusable `Section` with typed intro data and a surface tone, and `SectionHeading` for an eyebrow/title and optional supporting column. Keep the subtitle directly below the title at every breakpoint; never move it into a side column. Center the contact section heading, subtitle, and primary action; keep the expanded form text left-aligned for readability. Previous work and sample concepts have separate headings and data: never label the supplied websites as concepts or present concepts as delivered work. Vary ruled rows and restrained grids instead of making every section a card grid.
- Capability, process, work, and concept cards share `.image-card`: generous rounded corners, an image above a solid lightly graded text panel, a cool iridescent border, a fine inset highlight, and restrained layered shadows. The edge suggests laminated glass without placing blur or transparency behind card text. Use shared card-panel/ink/muted/rule/accent/radius/edge/shadow tokens for readable panels on either light or dark sections; avoid raw white/black overrides. Labels are optional and meaningful (such as work category or sample concept); do not number cards. Typed data supplies image, title, description, and an optional label. Keep one/two/three columns on mobile/tablet/desktop. On fine pointers, cards lift slightly on hover; actual links and disclosures give immediate press feedback and retain visible focus. Noninteractive cards have no pointer cursor or fake action arrows. Gate motion for reduced-motion users.
- The `Brand` component (`components/brand.tsx`) renders the logo mark plus a wordmark image, not live text. It ships two wordmark variants — `logo-wordmark-on-dark.png` (white "Temms") and `logo-wordmark-on-light.png` (ink "Temms") — swapped automatically via a `.dark-surface` CSS selector. Logo colours come from the theme: "Tech" is `--brand` cyan on `logo-wordmark-on-dark-v2.png` and `--visual-highlight` blue on `logo-wordmark-on-light-v2.png` (and the unreferenced `logo-wordmark.png`), "Temms" is `--ink` on light and off-white on dark, and `logo-mark-v2.png` keeps its cyan-to-blue gradient with the deep end lifted so it holds on both navy and white. Recolour the files, not the markup, if the palette changes, and give the new file a new name (the `-v2` suffix): Next.js caches optimised images by URL, so a same-name replacement can keep showing the old pixels. Don't reintroduce a text-based wordmark or hardcode one variant; add new surfaces to the same swap pattern if a third context appears.
- Controls use `rounded-md`; card surfaces use the shared card radius, layered edge, and shadow. Pills are for tags. Keep other surfaces restrained and avoid translucent glass except the sticky navbar.
- The hero pairs the company proposition with laptop/iPhone models. Every hero device shows the real websites, never screenshots: the laptop and tablet screens are a live browser window (tab strip, address bar, the real site) and the iPhone screen is a live mobile-width browser. Each website's tab opens in turn; the laptop turns front-on → to one side → back, alternating sides. Keep readable copy outside the canvas, cycle website previews automatically without selector buttons, captions, scroll prompts, or playback controls beneath the devices, and if WebGL fails show flat CSS device frames holding the same live sites. No screenshots or captures anywhere. The browser windows are not interactive (pointer events are off) and show a loading sheen until a site has loaded. Portfolio concepts remain explicitly labelled separately. Navbar links explicitly include Home, Capabilities, Our work, and Our approach; its contact action opens /contact. Use Next Link for internal routes and aria-current for the active page. Supporting pages use image-led cards and short supporting copy. Heroes vary by page: capability photo mosaic, overlapping work captures, immersive approach photograph, and a bright contact composition. Do not repeat homepage sections to fill these routes. Reuse tokens, controls, imagery, and verified project data where appropriate; keep stock photography decorative and never imply it depicts the team.
- Start with a single column. Navigation switches at `lg`, services at `sm/lg`, work at `md`, and process at `sm/lg`. Keep the hero columns top-aligned with compact top padding; avoid centering the copy within an oversized device stage. Verify 320px, tablet, desktop, and zoom; no fixed heights for text content.

## Interaction and accessibility

- Reuse shadcn primitives from `components/ui`. The Button supports native props, shared focus styles, and targets of at least 44px. Add primitives only for implemented needs.
- Buttons perform actions; links navigate. For `asChild`, pass one semantic child, and do not give links button-only attributes. Use native disabled states and meaningful accessible names.
- Preserve visible focus and WCAG AA contrast: 4.5:1 normal text, 3:1 large text and meaningful UI boundaries. Ink text on brand blue is intentional; white text on brand blue is not allowed.
- Root layout owns banner, main, footer, and skip link. Section modules use labelled sections and no nested main. The navbar hides when scrolling down and returns when scrolling up; hidden navigation is inert and excluded from the accessibility tree. The body uses the same ink background as the hero so hiding the sticky navbar cannot expose a light strip; each content section owns its surface color. Sticky navigation requires the global scroll offset. Mobile navigation is an inline disclosure, not a modal: announce expanded state, make closed content inert, close on selection/Escape, and restore trigger focus on Escape.
- Use native details/summary for sample descriptions and the project-brief composer. Do not replace semantics with clickable divs.
- The device showcase uses continuous gentle motion and a reversible screen-to-section transition. On desktop/tablet the laptop alone moves into the next section; below 768px the phone alone does. The other device is stationary at rest and leaves the transition scene as scrolling begins. Keep the preview attached to the active screen as the camera zooms until the screen covers the viewport; never expand it independently of the device. Hide the original section during the transition, restoring it at handoff and on cleanup. Finish when the real section reaches the viewport top, with its content matching the screen crop; explicit section links reveal the real section immediately. Keep native scrolling. Reduced motion disables automatic movement and zoom. Suspend rendering while hidden or offscreen. Other UI uses short transitions; do not use transition-all.
- After capabilities, the cards scroll upward naturally while the tablet rises edge-on from below, without covering the cards. It turns to face the viewer, then fills the viewport with the work section. The scroll interval includes the reserved runway and ends when the actual work section reaches the viewport top. Align the camera's final screen corner to the viewport origin so the preview stays fixed inside the device and matches the native section at handoff. Reverse the path on upward scrolling; explicit work links bypass the effect. Reserve the runway before assets load, removing it for reduced motion, unavailable models/WebGL, or JavaScript disabled.

- A contact action must explain what actually happens. The brief form collects one email-or-phone field, keeps its panel height fixed, and links the Privacy Policy under the button. Delivery lives in `modules/cta/submit-brief.ts`; it is simulated until the TODO there (route handler, Supabase, email alert) is built, and must be wired before launch so no real enquiry is lost.

Update this skill when recurring usage changes; numerical values remain in the theme.

The homepage's selected work starts with four cards in two columns from md up (one on mobile). Show more mounts the next batch only when requested, announces the count, and moves focus to the first newly revealed project. The Work route displays the complete collection. Keep this distinction in Portfolio/ProjectGrid props rather than duplicate layouts.

Supporting pages use SectionNav for a sticky, horizontally scrollable section index. Active links use aria-current="location" and a visible underline. Preserve native anchor scrolling, focus, browser history, reduced motion, and clearance below both navigation bars. Every substantive card has a relevant decorative image; screenshots represent real work while stock imagery must not imply actual team/client photography. Avoid autoplay or motion added purely to suggest novelty.

Hero devices retain a larger resting pose after model loading (camera framing owns the size, not CSS canvas scaling). Keep the projected screen handoff unchanged. Navbar page links and its home logo start at the destination top, including reselecting the current route; ordinary in-page links retain their section targets.

The homepage title and supporting paragraph cycle through content-managed messages with two letter scramble patterns and a readable pause. Keep exactly two visible title lines at every width, stable accessible names, and the original statement for reduced motion. Reserve the tallest description's space with static copies and keep the changing copy out of layout flow: scrambled letters can be wider and must not move scroll boundaries. Stop the animation when offscreen or the tab is hidden. Give the desktop 3D canvas room toward the viewport edge and pin its motion from the first scroll movement so the devices remain full-sized and visible until the screen takeover; preserve the screen target and full-viewport handoff.

The /approach page demonstrates the supplied square reveal between delivery, collaboration, and contact. An inert preview rises as a rounded square and opens to full viewport, then yields to the real section. It reverses on scroll up. Keep full content in normal document flow; skip the runway for reduced motion or missing JavaScript.

Ready animation zones pace large mouse-wheel and trackpad gestures in either direction, with immediate reversal and bounded travel. The document still uses native scrolling outside those zones. Keyboard navigation, touch scrolling, scrollbar dragging, explicit links, reduced motion, and failed models must stay usable without mandatory animation playback. Do not promise every navigation method plays every animation.

Use the shared theme easing for UI transitions. Gate decorative hover movement behind fine-pointer hover and no reduced-motion preference. Keep the sticky section navigation offset immediate rather than animating its layout position.

When the hero device transition is ready and motion is enabled, keep the real capabilities section hidden from the initial pose through the full-screen handoff, including reverse scrolling. Direct section navigation, reduced motion, and renderer failure must still expose real content.

Scroll pacing has one active owner across transition zones and retains fractional positions between frames. Ease into the target without overshooting. Navbar direction changes require accumulated travel so trackpad noise and edge bounce do not flicker the navigation.
